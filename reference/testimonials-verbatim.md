# Class Hero testimonials, transcribed verbatim

Source: the 27 images in `assets/live-site-originals/` (the testimonial screenshots on Bhavisha Valgi's current site). Transcribed 2026-09-26 by reading every image. The typed text on the developer's draft (`classhero-uk.vercel.app/` and `/results`) was used only to cross-check wording. Where the draft differs from a screenshot, the screenshot wins (see "Draft-site discrepancies" at the end).

## Rules used

- **Verbatim.** The author's spelling, grammar, capitalisation and punctuation are kept exactly, including mistakes ("alegbra", "lesssons", "definetely", "exam haul", "recommeneded", "never loose"). Nothing is smoothed. Emojis are dropped; text emoticons such as `:)` are kept because they are typed characters.
- **Redactions stay redacted.** A name blurred or whited out in the screenshot is written `[name]`. Names that are visible in the screenshot are transcribed, but see "Do not use" for children's names and consent.
- **Client side only.** For WhatsApp threads only the parent's or student's bubbles are quoted. Bhavisha's own messages (green bubbles with blue ticks, i.e. sent from her phone) are included only where the client's reply makes no sense without them, and are marked **[Bhavisha, context]**.
- **Tutor-reported results are flagged.** Several grade claims (Bhumi 5→7, Phoebe 30-40%→75%, the 78% perimeter task) are written by Bhavisha, not by the client. They are real data but they are not testimonials, and the site must not present them as a parent or student speaking.
- `[illegible]` marks anything unreadable. `[cropped]` marks text cut off by the screenshot edge.
- Superprof reviews show only a first name; that is how they are attributed.

## Strongest 8 for the homepage

1. **#26 Taleen.** A Grade 1 student who cried in her first lessons, passed GCSE Maths and is now doing A-Levels. Her own words, full arc, strong emotion. Best single quote in the set.
2. **#18 Grade 6 to 8 in 5 weeks.** The student's own claim, a specific number and timeframe, and it names the method (repeating similar questions). Needs a first name or "GCSE Maths student" as attribution; the name is blurred.
3. **#1 Nitin Parmar.** A named referee with a charity connection who also became a paying parent. The only full-name endorsement, and the letter carries weight as a character reference.
4. **#4 Higher-tier email.** "I used to think I could never do it": a student moved up to the Higher paper, and the topic list makes it specific and credible. Use the highlighted section ("Regardless I wanted to say thank you ... thank you so much.").
5. **#14 Jennifer.** A parent's outcome claim: "underachieving to above average in a year", plus confidence and enjoyment. Short enough for a card.
6. **#32 Rosa B → A, Mona C → B.** Parent-supplied before and after grades for two Year 8 students in about a month. Quote only the parent's words ("Rosa B to A / Mona C to B", "A month back"); the "4 weeks" framing is Bhavisha's.
7. **#11 Pearl.** A Year 8 student naming the topic she found hardest (algebra), the tutor's philosophy ("Nothing is ever difficult, only foreign") and the effect on her anxiety. Specific and human. Typos must be kept or the quote trimmed around them.
8. **#29 Nelson.** A parent of a Year 11 and a Year 8 who describes the method (notes, speaking up, homework) and reports "a leap in their grades in school". Good for the parent audience.
